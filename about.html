Why a response drone?

The design brief, operating requirements, and research behind HARD.

The problem

Arriving blind.
Deciding fast.

When a call comes in for a fire, a flood, a building collapse, or a missing person, the first crews on scene often arrive with little more than the dispatcher’s description. Ground units are slowed by traffic, terrain, and debris, and some scenes are too dangerous to walk into. Drone-as-first-responder programs show the value of getting a camera over an incident in the first minutes, but most rely on larger camera drones built for steady hovering rather than speed and agility. Responders need a portable, low-cost aircraft that can launch on demand, cover distance quickly, maneuver close to structures, and send live video back to the people making decisions.

Proposed approach

Built for speed.
Flown for awareness.

HARD should be a high-speed FPV drone that a trained pilot flies through a low-latency live video link. It should hold a steady position for observation, return home automatically if the link or battery runs low, and carry a thermal camera to see through smoke and at night. FPGA integration should support onboard processing that helps the operator spot people and hazards. Simulation and testing should guide the final configuration, and outdoor flights should follow FAA rules.

Design requirements

What the vehicle should do.

  • Fly fast
  • Hover steadily for observation
  • Prioritize speed and stability
  • Launch quickly
  • Stream low-latency live video
  • Support thermal imaging
  • Return home automatically
  • Support FPGA integration
  • Support remote control
